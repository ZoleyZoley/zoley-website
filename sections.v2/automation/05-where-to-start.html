a rule we use on every audit

Not everything is worth automating. Here's how we decide.

Owners often arrive with a list of ten things they'd like to automate. We'll help you figure out prioritization by scoring the list with three questions and build the one with the highest return first. The rest wait until the first one has paid for itself.

The right first build is different for every business, and it's usually smaller than people expect.

The three questions

  1. How often does it happen? Daily beats weekly. Weekly beats monthly. Below monthly, a template usually wins.
  2. What does it cost when it's late or missed? A slow lead reply costs revenue. A late internal report costs almost nothing.
  3. Is the process stable? If the steps change every month, we document first and automate second.
High frequency, high cost of delay, stable process: that's your first build.
