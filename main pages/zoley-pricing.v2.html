how pricing works

Priced per engagement. Agreed in writing before we start.

We don't publish a rate card, because the honest answer to "what does it cost?" depends on what needs to be built. A pricing review and a full automation build are different amounts of work, and pretending otherwise would mean overcharging one client to subsidize another.

What we do publish is the process. Every engagement is scoped to specific deliverables with a fixed price, set before any work begins. No hourly meters, no retainers you can't cancel, and no invoice for something you didn't agree to.

what shapes the number

Three things determine what an engagement costs.

Once we've heard about your business, the quote is arrived at from these. We'll show our reasoning, and if the number doesn't fit your budget, we'll suggest a smaller first step rather than a smaller version of the same thing.

One rule we hold to: we quote the first engagement small enough to prove itself. A build that returns its cost inside a quarter earns the next one. That's better for you, and it's how we've kept most clients for years.
  • 1

    Scope of the build

    How many systems are involved, and how much of the work is research versus construction. A pricing review is a different job from a site rebuild with automation wired in.

  • 2

    Where you're starting from

    Documented processes and clean data make a build faster. If we need to document how the business runs before we can automate it, that's part of the scope, and we'll say so.

  • 3

    Support after launch

    Every build includes a 30-day review and handover. Ongoing support is optional and priced separately, so you're never paying for a retainer you don't use.
