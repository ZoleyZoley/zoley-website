a letter from our founder

Every business has a story. Here's ours.

You won't find a list of services on this page, and that's on purpose. Every business we work with has a story behind it, and you should know the one behind ours before you trust us with yours.

Zoley didn't start with a business plan. It started with a layoff.

For years I worked inside a Fortune 50 company, leading operational teams across different parts of the business. I learned how a large organization actually runs: how work moves from one team to the next, where it stalls, and how much a small fix in the right place is worth. Then I was laid off, and I had to figure out what came next.

The answer was closer than I expected. Some of my friends are exceptional at what they do. They're physical therapists and dentists with patients who love them. But nobody teaches the business side in physical therapy or dental school: the pricing, the scheduling, the follow-up with new patients, the systems that keep a practice running while you're busy doing the actual work. They were carrying all of that on top of their real jobs, mostly by hand.

I had spent my career on exactly that side of the house. So I started helping, one problem at a time. Zoley grew out of necessity, not a grand plan: a next step for me, and a need I kept seeing in the businesses around me.

I'm naturally curious, and I like to build things. When I look at a business, my first question isn't which software to sell it. It's how the work actually happens today. More often than not, the right answer is something built for that business specifically: an intake process that fits how the front desk really works, an automation that connects two tools that were never meant to talk, a report the owner actually reads. Off-the-shelf tools have their place, but most businesses don't run the way the software assumes they do.

The name Zoley comes from my grandfather. Whenever he came to visit, he'd settle in with the crossword from the newspaper and a bowl of pistachios or tortilla chips next to him, working through the clues one square at a time. That's where my love of puzzles comes from, and it's still how I think about this work. Every business is its own puzzle, made from the same kinds of pieces: people, tools, time and money. My job is to work out how they fit, fill in what's missing, and keep at it until the whole thing makes sense.

That's why this page reads like a letter instead of a brochure. Your business has its own story too, and I'd like to hear it.

Ryan K. PhelanFounder, Zoley Consulting
