a quick self-audit

Five signs your site is working against you.

These are the issues we find most often in the first ten minutes of a site audit. If two or more apply, a rebuild usually pays for itself within the year.

  • 01

    It was built years ago, and it looks it.

    Visitors read a dated site as a signal about the business behind it, fairly or not.

  • 02

    You don't turn up when people search for you.

    If the page titles, descriptions, and structure were never set up behind the scenes, search engines have almost nothing to go on. The site is live; it just isn't findable.

  • 03

    It's slow, or it breaks on a phone.

    Most of your visitors are on mobile. If the layout shifts, the buttons are tiny, or it stalls, they're gone.

  • 04

    A stranger can't tell what you do in five seconds.

    Vague headlines and industry jargon send people back to the search results.

  • 05

    There's no obvious next step.

    If the visitor has to hunt for how to book, buy, or reach you, most won't. One clear action per page.

tracking & reporting

You'll know whether it's working, in numbers.

A website without measurement is a guess with a domain name. We can set up analytics around the actions that matter to your business, and send a monthly summary written in plain language.

These are the six numbers we can report on. If one of them isn't moving, that's where the next improvement goes.

Qualified inquiries

Form submissions, calls, and bookings that came from the site, not just raw visits.

Conversion rate

The share of visitors who take the action the page was built for.

Load time

Measured on a mid-range phone over a normal connection, which is how most visitors arrive.

Traffic by source

Search, referrals, ads, and direct, so you know which channel is earning its keep.

Top pages & exits

Where visitors go, and the page they were on when they left.

Search visibility

The terms you appear for and where, tracked month over month.

case study

What a rebuild looks like in practice.

40%
more interest in their listings after the rebuild

A real estate brokerage handing its buyers to Zillow.

Buyers were arriving on the site and leaving within a click, because every listing pointed out to Zillow and most of them never came back. We restructured the pages around the path we wanted a buyer to take, fixed the search setup so the brokerage could actually be found, and brought every listing onto the site itself instead of linking away. Buyers who landed now had a reason to stay, and the interest stayed with the brokerage instead of going to a portal.
